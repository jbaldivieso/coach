Coach looks, round two

Your feedback: the category standard was the clearest but needs its own color and type. The pad had character but its hierarchy was too flat. So all four looks below keep the standard look's structure (cards, clear headings, huge numbers for the current set) and differ in palette, type and a few treatments.

Each is shown on Home, Plan and Track using your own Jan 2026 data. The category standard is last, as the baseline.

Safety signal

Gym equipment and floor markings: safety yellow, black, stenciled DIN-style type.

Uses the standard look's structure. One safety yellow marks what's live: the app bar, the current set and the Done button. Everything else is black on a cool concrete gray.

It belongs in a gym, and yellow on black reads at arm's length under any lighting. The yellow only ever means 'this one, now', so the hierarchy reads from across the room.

Type: Saira

Risk: If yellow spreads beyond 'live' it starts to feel like a hazard sign. It needs discipline.

Sports page

Newspaper sports section: Franklin Gothic headlines, agate box-score tables, a red section flag.

The paper log's love of numbers, given a proper newspaper hierarchy: heavy headlines, thick-and-thin rules, and dense box-score sets underneath.

It fixes the pad's flatness with real typographic steps (headline, deck, table). The box-score style suits history and the last-3 comparison.

Type: Libre Franklin

Risk: Newsprint and rules can tip into nostalgia or 'editorial' if the rules are too fussy.

Whiteboard

The gym's own whiteboard: white enamel, aluminum frame, marker blue/red/green, round magnets.

White enamel with marker colors doing defined jobs: blue for structure, red for 'now', green for 'done'. Round magnet pills for the exercise strip.

Lifters plan on whiteboards. It's as clear as the standard look but with its own color language, and the marker colors carry state instead of decorating.

Type: Rubik

Risk: Three colors need strict roles, or it turns into a children's app.
