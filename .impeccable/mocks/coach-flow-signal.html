Coach flow, in Safety signal

The full flow from the wireframes in the look you picked. Yellow means live, act now: the app bar, the current set, Done, and the rest screen. Everything else is black on concrete gray. Saira throughout, condensed caps for names and labels.

Set chips now sit on a fixed six-column grid, since no exercise goes past 6 sets. Chips get more room, and set N lines up with set N in the rows above and below, so comparing outings is a straight vertical read.

Home

The calendar stays at the top so you can decide whether to train today. You start a session from here, and it doesn't guess what's next.

Plan

Today's targets go next to the last three times you did each exercise, looked up by exercise name and pulled from your data, not pasted into comments. You change whatever you want, and nothing progresses on its own.

Track

Shows one exercise or superset at a time. Done logs what's on screen and starts the rest timer. A planned target is replaced by what you actually did when you log it, and only actual sets are kept.

History

Each kind of question gets its own way in. Tap an exercise name anywhere to see its history. Tap the month to browse the year. Search covers "when did I last…".

Rules this look runs on

  • Yellow is a signal, not a brand color. It marks the app bar, the current set, the primary action, a target you've changed in Plan, and the whole rest screen. A selected month and the newest point on a chart may also use it. It should never decorate.
  • Black means "this is state". Trained days, the current exercise chip, superset headers, the Resume bar and search answers are black. White cards hold content, and concrete gray is the ground.
  • Condensed caps are for names, plain text for data. Exercise and session names are set condensed, uppercase. Numbers and comments stay in regular-width Saira so they read easily.
  • ⋯ everywhere. One menu button in the same spot on every screen holds What's new and Log out.
  • Superset = hazard tape. The only striped element in the app marks exercises that are linked together.
  • Name drift (still open). "Chin ups" and "Chin-ups" are separate exercises unless names are matched ignoring case and punctuation.
