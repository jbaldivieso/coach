Coach, flow wireframes

Grayscale on purpose: these mocks settle what's on each screen and how you get between them. Visual identity comes later. The numbers come from your own log (Jan 2026), with "today" set to Fri Jan 23.

The app treats exercise names as plain text. It makes no guesses about equipment and draws no pictograms. Names are kept consistent only by autocomplete, and history is looked up by exact name.

Home

The calendar stays at the top so you can decide whether to train today. You start a session from here, and it doesn't guess what's next.

Plan

Today's targets go next to the last three times you did each exercise, looked up by exercise name and pulled from your data, not pasted into comments. You change whatever you want, and nothing progresses on its own.

Track

Shows one exercise or superset at a time. Done logs what's on screen and starts the rest timer. A planned target is replaced by what you actually did when you log it, and only actual sets are kept.

History

Each kind of question gets its own way in. Tap an exercise name anywhere to see its history. Tap the month to browse the year. Search covers "when did I last…".

Notes for review

  • Name drift is real. Your log has "Chin ups" and "Chin-ups", and "Incline Bench" alongside "Incline press". History looks up the exact name, so these show up as separate exercises. The autocomplete counts are meant to nudge you toward one spelling. One option: match names ignoring case and punctuation, so "Chin ups" and "Chin-ups" merge without the app guessing anything else.
  • Sets are shown one by one. Every set appears as its own weight × reps, everywhere, so a change in weight partway through an exercise is always visible.
  • Skipping and reordering in Track. Tapping an exercise in the progress strip jumps to it. Changing the order mid-session would go through a menu, so a stray drag can't reorder things.
  • No tab bar. Home is the hub, and history is reached by tapping things. With so few places to go, a tab bar would mostly be chrome.
